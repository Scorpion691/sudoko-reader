Sudoku Reader · P7The problem

One misread digit breaks the whole puzzle

  • The network is right 97.8% of the time per digit.
  • A puzzle has about 30 digits, and all of them must be right.
  • If errors were independent, about half of the puzzles would contain a misread (0.97830 ≈ 0.51).
  • The solver then says “no solution” and gives no hint where the error is.
Goal: find the least unlikely corrections that make the puzzle solvable again.

Sudoku Reader · P7Search

Search over correction sets

  • State: a set of corrections {(cell, digit)}
  • Cost: log(pread / pnew), never negative
  • Goal: the CSP solves the corrected puzzle
  • Branching: 15 least-confident cells × 2 alternatives
h = max over conflicts (a, b) of min( m(a), m(b) )

Each duplicate pair needs one of its two cells changed, so the cheapest fix of the hardest conflict is a lower bound: h never overestimates.

Sudoku Reader · P7Limits and conclusions

What works, and what does not yet

Conclusions
  • Recovery 0.96 / 0.77 / 0.55 for 1 / 2 / 3 misreads, 0.00 without correction
  • A* = UCS quality with far fewer nodes
  • FC + AC-3 cut CSP work by up to 86%
Limitations
  • Misreads are injected; MNIST digits are cleaner than real handwriting
  • “Solvable” accepts a cheaper wrong puzzle (the unique-solution goal helps: 72% → 88% in a diagnostic run)
  • 5 seeds × 20 puzzles: large variance
